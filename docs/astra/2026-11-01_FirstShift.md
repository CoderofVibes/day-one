### A1. Put explanation where it is useful

The current shell requires a descriptive sentence on every page (`web/src/shell.tsx:275`); the shared page registry supplies most of them (`web/src/App.tsx:217`). Use this rule consistently:

| Information | Its home | Concrete treatment |
|---|---|---|
| What this page is for | Usually the title; optional page help | Delete a sentence that merely repeats the title or enumerates visible sections. Do not automatically migrate every subtitle into a tooltip. |
| What to do with a particular control | Control label; first-use empty state; keyboard-accessible help | “Find a lot”, “Review draft”, “Upload batch record”. Keep instructions beside an empty queue when an action is actually needed. |
| Why a decision is needed and its consequences | The decision itself | Keep the desk's three questions, payment conditions, refused-email reason and signing consequences visible. These are working information. |
| How Kevin and the program work | First-shift walkthrough; “Help with this page” | A help panel has at most three short sections: purpose, actions, limits/source. It is available without replaying the introduction. |
| Whether a number is recorded, generated, simulated or modeled | Beside that number or panel | A compact source/date label stays visible. Details open on click or keyboard focus. Never hide provenance just to shorten a page. |
| Replay restrictions | Beside the affected action | “Recorded preview · signing unavailable” or “Recorded questions”. Developer setup instructions belong in help, behind the curtain or the README. |
| Errors, uncertainty, refused actions | At the affected record | Do not demote these into help, tooltips or a welcome script. |

## B. Kevin's introduction

### B1. Three viable forms and the choice

1. **The line — recommended.** A fine green line rests in the middle of an otherwise quiet screen. Small waves travel through it while a caption is presented; it becomes still between captions. This extends the existing green K identity without suggesting a human face, a microphone or a new chat product. Risk: an unlabelled waveform resembles audio recording; always show **Kevin**, never a microphone, input meter or “listening” state. S–M.
2. **A small avatar.** A 40×40 SVG rounded square, radius 12, uses `--brand-soft`; two 3 px ink circles sit at (14,17) and (26,17), with a 10 px, 2 px-stroke horizontal mouth at y=26. During a caption the mouth expands vertically by at most 2 px; eyes do not track the pointer. It is approachable, but gives a serious operations assistant a mascot personality and needs more taste decisions. M; weaker than the line for this build.
3. **The marked page.** A 36×44 outlined sheet, radius 4, with three 18 px rules and a 6 px folded corner. Each caption lights one rule in green; at completion the page becomes the icon on **Morning review**. This fits the paperwork benefit and can be drawn in SVG, but risks making Kevin look like a document feature rather than an assistant across the plant. S–M; useful fallback if motion is cut.

Choose **the line**, with the existing K mark retained in navigation. Do not commission a raster avatar, generate imagery, add particle effects or introduce a new font. The character comes from Kevin's precise, restrained words and his prepared work.

### B2. Exact form and motion

New **`web/src/KevinPresence.tsx`** renders an SVG with `viewBox="0 0 240 48"`, width 240 px and height 48 px; phone width 200 px. Its line runs from x=8 to x=232 around y=24, stroke `var(--brand)`, stroke-width 2.5, round caps, no fill, shadow or glow. Keep **Kevin** below it at 14/20 px, weight 600. The SVG is decorative; Kevin's name and captions carry the meaning.

For the presenting state, sample 17 points along the line at equal intervals and join smoothly. For normalized position `u` from 0 to 1, use `y = 24 + 7*sin(pi*u)^2*sin(4*pi*u - 2*pi*t/1600)`, with time in milliseconds. The end points stay level. Ease amplitude from 0 to 7 px over 180 ms at the start of a caption, hold for at most 2.4 seconds, then back to zero over 180 ms. This is deterministic caption motion, not an audio visualization. Stop the animation loop while still, hidden or unmounted.

States:

- **Idle:** straight line, 75% opacity, no breathing loop.
- **Presenting caption:** full opacity and the finite wave above. Subsequent reading time is still.
- **Finished:** straight line, full opacity. Do not add a green check that could imply all plant work is complete.
- **Reduced motion:** straight line in every state; captions and program visibility cut directly. The same controls remain.

After the intro, the walkthrough uses a 24 px existing K mark and a short 40 px still line beside Kevin's name. Do not shrink the full wave into an illegible tiny animation. No decorative motion in the normal header; its status continues to come from the existing activity model (`Header.tsx:110`). Intro animation must never report “working”, “scanning” or “connected” as if a real job were happening.
